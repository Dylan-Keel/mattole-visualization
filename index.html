    What this covers

    Twenty-two dates, October 1941 to July 2025. Every frame is on the same UTM zone 10N grid, so the flip is registered. The watershed view is 2.4 by 2.7 km at 1 m per pixel. The parcel view is 1.12 by 1.54 km at 0.5 m per pixel.

    1941 through 1981 are full-resolution scans. These nine dates are mosaics of scanned prints from Cal Poly Humboldt, about 0.5 to 1 m per pixel at source. Individual tree crowns, skid roads and landings are visible. I orthorectified each frame against the 10 m 3DEP elevation model by matching the photo's terrain shading to a simulated hillshade. Fit residuals are about 7 to 12 m, so features shift slightly between years, more on steep ground. Years built from two flight lines show a seam, and some prints carry grease-pencil marks.

    1993 and 1998 are single USGS National Aerial Photography Program frames at about 1.7 m, orthorectified with 2005 NAIP as control. Chips with dashed borders mark all black-and-white dates.

    Color balance varies by year in the NAIP frames and I left the tones as delivered. Canopy texture, openings and road lines are the reliable signal.

    3D view. Each year is draped on the 10 m elevation model and rendered from one fixed camera at 4,976 ft, facing due south with a 20 degree downward tilt. The terrain model is smooth at this resolution, so gullies and road cuts do not show as relief, and steep slopes stretch the photos somewhat.

    Gaps. 1974 covers the east side only and 1966 misses the northeast corner. I found 1960 frames for the west half only and left that year out because 1959 is complete. I could not place any 1958, 1984 or 1987 frames over the catchment.

    Parcel boundary is from Humboldt County GIS. The watershed divide is delineated from the 10 m elevation model. Stream lines are from the National Hydrography Dataset. Wild Turkey Creek is the owner's name for this unnamed Mattole tributary. None of these lines is a survey.

    Fire record. The optional fire layer shows CAL FIRE historical perimeters. Two fires cover most of this ground: the C. Gardner Fire of September 3, 1955 (1,759 acres) and the Mattole Fire of September 23, 1964 (16,493 acres). A perimeter drawn dashed burned after the date on screen. Older perimeters were often sketched by hand at coarse scale, and the record misses small and early fires, so treat edges as approximate.
